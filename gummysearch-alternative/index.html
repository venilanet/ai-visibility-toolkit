GummySearch Is Shutting Down (Dec 2026): 6 Alternatives Compared

If you do audience research or lead generation on Reddit, you have probably heard the news: GummySearch is shutting down on December 1, 2026, and all user data will be deleted that day. With more than 140,000 founders, marketers, and investors relying on it, the shutdown leaves a real gap — and a deadline.

This guide covers what exactly is happening, what to look for in a replacement, and how six alternatives compare on price, features, and free trials — so you can migrate before your data disappears.

What Happened to GummySearch?

GummySearch was a Reddit audience research tool: you picked communities, searched what people wrote in them, and tracked keywords over time. In November 2025, its maker announced the end. The reason was access to Reddit data — in the company's own words, it could not reach an agreement that aligns with Reddit's Data API usage policies, which forbid commercial applications without a commercial license.

Here is the official timeline:

What this means for you: if you still have access, export your saved audiences, keyword lists, and anything else you want to keep before December 1, 2026. Free-tier users can still log in to view, download, or delete their data — all other functionality is disabled.

What to Look for in a Replacement

No single tool replaces everything GummySearch did, so the smartest way to choose is by job. Most people used GummySearch for one or more of these three jobs:

  1. Audience research — finding pain points and patterns across subreddits.
  2. Keyword alerts — getting notified when someone mentions your brand, a competitor, or a problem you solve.
  3. Finding threads to reply to — spotting "what tool should I use?" posts while they are still fresh.

Before comparing tools, write down which of the three you actually used. Then evaluate each alternative on:

  • Reddit coverage depth — does it scan all subreddits, or only a subset?
  • Alert speed — real-time alerts vs. delayed digests (matters a lot if you reply to threads).
  • AI filtering/synthesis — does it cut noise with AI, or dump raw mentions on you?
  • Data export — can you get your keyword lists and history out?
  • Price and free trial — GummySearch cost $29–$199/month depending on tier; know your budget.
  • Longevity — after this shutdown, pick a tool with a sustainable data-access story.

1. Syften — best for keyword alerts across many communities

Syften monitors Reddit, Hacker News, X, Indie Hackers, forums, Stack Exchange, GitHub, YouTube, podcasts, and the wider web — the broadest community coverage on this list. Entry is $29.95/month with three community filters; Standard at $49.95/month adds AI filtering and Slack integration. It describes itself as a GummySearch alternative specifically for keyword monitoring rather than audience research.

Choose Syften if: your main GummySearch job was keyword alerts and you want them from many communities, not just Reddit.

2. F5Bot — best free option

F5Bot has been running since 2017 and does one thing well: keyword alerts from Reddit, Hacker News, and Lobsters. The free plan covers the basics with no time limit. Silver ($9.99/month) raises the limit to 20 keywords; Gold ($49.99/month) adds AI filtering, Slack/Discord delivery, and RSS/JSON feeds.

Choose F5Bot if: you are a solo founder who just needs to know when a keyword shows up — and you like free.

3. Reddinbox — best for market research + AI synthesis

Reddinbox covers Reddit, X, Bluesky, Hacker News, and Facebook, with AI synthesis of what it finds — the closest in spirit to GummySearch's research workflow. Plans start at $39/month with a free trial that does not require a credit card. Note it does not offer real-time alerts, so it suits research better than rapid thread replies.

Choose Reddinbox if: your main GummySearch job was audience research and pain-point discovery rather than instant outreach.

4. Redreach — Reddit lead generation on a budget

Redreach is Reddit-specific and focused on lead generation rather than broad research. Starting at $19/month with a free trial, it is one of the cheapest paid options here. AI capabilities are limited compared to pricier tools, but if your workflow is "find buying-intent threads on Reddit and reply," it covers the essentials.

Choose Redreach if: you want the cheapest paid path to Reddit lead alerts and do not need multi-platform coverage.

5. Awario — solid mid-range brand monitoring

Awario monitors 10+ channels with real-time alerts, starting at $29/month on annual billing ($49 month-to-month). It is a general social-listening tool rather than a Reddit specialist — a good fit if your monitoring needs extend beyond Reddit to the broader web and social media.

Choose Awario if: you need brand monitoring across many channels at a mid-range price.

6. Brand24 — enterprise-grade monitoring

Brand24 covers 25+ channels with AI features and real-time alerts, starting at $199/month (annual billing; $249 month-to-month). It is the most expensive option here and aimed at teams and agencies rather than solo founders. If you outgrew GummySearch's scope and need serious brand monitoring, this is the step up.

Choose Brand24 if: you are a team or agency that needs enterprise-grade monitoring and the budget to match.

Migration Checklist: Do This Before December 1

  1. Export your GummySearch data now. Saved audiences, keyword lists, historical searches — download everything while you still can. Do not assume it will be there in November.
  2. Document your current workflow. Write down which keywords you track, which subreddits matter, and what you do with each alert. You will rebuild this in the new tool.
  3. Trial two alternatives side by side for one week. Feed them the same 5–10 keywords and compare alert quality, noise level, and speed. One week of parallel running beats hours of feature-matrix reading.
  4. Move your keyword lists and alert destinations (email, Slack) to the winner. Keep GummySearch running until the switch is verified.
  5. Cancel before your renewal date if you are on a paid plan — there is no point paying for a tool with a known shutdown date.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is GummySearch really shutting down?

Yes. New signups closed on November 30, 2025, and the service shuts down entirely on December 1, 2026, when all user data is deleted. The cause is Reddit's API policy, which forbids commercial applications without a commercial license.

Will my GummySearch data be deleted?

Yes — all user data is deleted on December 1, 2026, according to the official announcement. Export your saved audiences and keyword lists well before that date.

Can I still sign up for GummySearch?

No. Signups have been closed since November 30, 2025, with no exceptions. Existing customers keep access until their billing period ends.

What is the best free GummySearch alternative?

F5Bot. Its free plan covers basic keyword alerts from Reddit, Hacker News, and Lobsters with no time limit — the closest free replacement for GummySearch's alerting job.

Which alternative is closest to what GummySearch did?

None replaces it exactly. Reddinbox is closest for audience research with AI synthesis; Syften is closest for real-time keyword monitoring across communities. Pick based on which of GummySearch's three jobs (research, alerts, thread-finding) you used most.

Should I migrate now or wait?

Migrate now. Run a one-week parallel trial of your top two candidates with the same keywords, then move over. Waiting until late November risks rushed decisions and lost historical context.
