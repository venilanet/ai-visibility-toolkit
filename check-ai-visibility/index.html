How to Check Where Your Site Stands in ChatGPT, Perplexity & AI Overviews

Buyers increasingly ask AI assistants for recommendations instead of scrolling Google results. The question every site owner should be asking: when someone asks AI about my category, does it cite me — or my competitors? Here's how to check, free methods first.

Free Methods First: The Prompt-Panel Technique

Before spending on any tool, run this manual check. It takes 15–30 minutes and answers the core question directly.

Step 1: Build your prompt panel (10–20 variations)

Write the questions your buyers actually ask — not branded queries about you, but category questions where a recommendation could go to anyone:

  • "What's the best [category] tool for a small team?"
  • "[Competitor] vs [Competitor] — which should I choose?"
  • "Is there a free alternative to [category leader]?"
  • "How do I solve [specific pain point]?"

Aim for 10–20 variations covering informational ("how do I…"), comparison ("X vs Y"), and transactional ("best … for …") intents.

Step 2: Run them clean

Use a fresh/incognito session where possible to reduce personalization. Ask each prompt once in ChatGPT, once in Perplexity, and Google the equivalent query for AI Overviews. Do not cherry-pick — run the whole panel the same way every time.

Step 3: Score every answer the same way

For each prompt × each surface, record one of four outcomes:

  1. Recommended — the AI actively suggests your product. (This is the money outcome.)
  2. Cited — your page is linked/referenced as a source.
  3. Mentioned — your brand name appears but isn't endorsed or linked.
  4. Absent — neither you nor your URL appears.

Log it in a spreadsheet with the date. One check means little — AI answers vary day to day. Four weekly checks reveal the trend.

What Google Search Console can't tell you: GSC reports impressions and clicks but does not break out AI Overviews as a separate search appearance. If your impressions rise, you can't tell from GSC alone whether AI Overviews drove it. The manual method above fills that blind spot.

When a Paid Tool Is Worth It

The manual method stops scaling at around 30–50 prompts, or when you need daily data and competitor comparisons. That's when a tracker earns its keep. The decision rule is simple:

  • Stay manual if you track <30 prompts, check weekly, and only care about your own brand.
  • Consider paid if you track 50+ prompts, need daily sampling (AI answers are noisy — single runs mislead), or want automated competitor-gap reports.
  • Price reality check: budget trackers start around €20–30/month; serious mid-tier tools run ~€89/month; enterprise platforms go far beyond. Never pay enterprise prices to answer a 20-prompt question.

How to Read the Results: Citation ≠ Mention ≠ Recommendation

This distinction is where most people misread their data:

  • Citation: your URL is referenced as a source. Good — it means your content is in the AI's retrieval set. But users don't always click citations.
  • Named mention: your brand appears in the prose ("…tools like [You], [Competitor A]…"). Weak signal on its own.
  • Recommendation: the AI tells the user to try you ("For small teams, I'd suggest [You] because…"). This is what drives trials and purchases.

Being named in passing while the AI recommends three competitors is not a win. When you score your prompt panel, weight recommendations 10× higher than mentions. If competitors are recommended where you're merely mentioned, that's your gap to close — usually with better comparison content and third-party citations, not more blog posts.

A Simple 15-Minute Weekly Routine

  1. Run your prompt panel (10–20 prompts) across ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google — same prompts, same order, weekly. (10 min)
  2. Score and log each result as recommended / cited / mentioned / absent, with the date. (3 min)
  3. Note changes: which prompts flipped vs. last week? New competitor appearing? You disappearing somewhere you were cited? (2 min)
  4. Monthly: tally the 4-week trend per prompt. Only act on patterns across 3+ weeks — single-week flips are usually noise.

Do this for a month before buying any tool. You'll either find you don't need one — or you'll know exactly which features to pay for.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check if my website appears in ChatGPT answers?

Ask ChatGPT your buyers' actual questions (10–20 variations) in a fresh session and record whether your brand is cited, mentioned, recommended, or absent in each answer. Repeat weekly and track trends.

Can Google Search Console show AI Overviews data?

Not separately. GSC reports overall impressions and clicks without breaking out AI Overviews, so manual checking is currently the only way to attribute visibility specifically to AI Overviews.

How often should I check?

Weekly. AI answers are noisy — the same prompt can cite different sources on different days — so look at 4-week trends, not single checks.

Citation vs. mention — what's the difference?

A citation links your page as a source; a mention just names your brand; a recommendation actively endorses you. Recommendations drive business; mentions mostly don't.

Do I need a paid tool?

Not initially. The manual panel method is free and sufficient under ~30 prompts. Pay when you need daily data, 50+ prompts, or automated competitor analysis.
